How to play

Command view

Pick a tower or a factory building from the bar (number keys; F switches between Towers and Factory), then click a free hex to build. Shift keeps building.

Click something you built to upgrade (U) or sell (X) it: towers, and Transfer Arms, mines, pumpjacks, plants and generators too. R turns a selected arm or belt. T steps a selected tower through its targeting modes (Shift+T backwards). The tags over the spawn gates show what the next wave sends down each road.

Right-click a charged tower to fire its special: it does not have to be selected. WASD or right-drag to pan, wheel to zoom. Q E or middle-drag turns the view, Home resets it.

N calls the next wave. Between waves time stands still: build, nothing is made and nothing moves until you call (or Auto waves calls for you). G switches the game speed, P or Esc pauses.

Tap a building in the bar, then tap a hex. Hold and drag to move the preview first (a belt or a line of arms follows your finger). Tap something on the board to upgrade or sell it. Hold a charged tower, or double-tap it, to fire its special.

Drag with one finger to pan. Two fingers either pinch to zoom or twist to turn the view. Between waves time stands still until you tap Call wave, unless you switch Auto waves on.

Supplying towers

A tower fires only what it is fed. It starts empty and keeps just one item in hand. Nothing hands anything on by itself: every item that passes from a mine, a plant, a belt or a crate into a tower, a building or onto a belt is carried by a Transfer Arm. The one exception keeps the first minute simple: a supply crate feeds the towers built right next to it, until it runs out.

An arm takes one tile. It takes from the tile behind it and delivers to the tile in front of it, straight through; the preview shows the direction, R turns it, and dragging lays a line of arms that hand on to each other, Turn changes it, and hold and drag lays a line of arms that hand on to each other. It only picks up what its target will take right now, so nothing jams in its hand. ClickTap an arm to give it a filter: one kind of item only, to pull that kind off a mixed belt.

A Mine digs about one ore every 3 seconds, enough for one Pulse Turret: Mine, arm, turret in a straight line is a whole supply line (point attap a deposit to see what it is). Belts come with the Conveyor Belts card, always among the first offers. A belt only carries: an arm puts items on it, other arms take them off further along, so one belt can pass a row of towers and each arm decides what its tower gets. A belt pointing into another merges onto it. Pipes and power keep it simple: a pipe feeds what it touches, a Generator or Power Pole powers what stands within 2 tiles.

Between waves a broken rust ring under a tower or building, with a hologram of the missing item over it, means it will lack something when the wave starts; clicktap it to see what is missing, which hand-off is not there and what supplies it. The Power gauge shows what the grids need against what they have.

While you place something, a green ring marks everything that will be supplied, dashes flow along the route the new piece would get, and brackets close on what it would feed or power. Hold V to see the green rings at any time between waves, or switch them on for good with the hexagon button beside Auto waves.The hexagon button beside Auto waves keeps the green rings on between waves.

Every building is on the Factory bar from the start; a hatched one is locked and names the card that unlocks it. Arms, mines, pumpjacks, plants and generators upgrade twice to work faster.

Cards, specials and the machines

Every level you pick one of three cards: tower blueprints, production cards, tower upgrades, factory upgrades or economy. A blueprint unlocks only its tower; the buildings that make its supply come with production cards of their own. A card is offered only once what it builds on is there (a Shell Press needs Smelting and the Power Grid), a blueprint card says which production cards its tower still waits for, and those are put into the next offers. The paths below show what needs what.

A Pulse Turret Mk3 can fire plasma shells and a Firewall Mk3 can burn petrol, for more damage. Upgrading never changes what a tower eats: the Plasma Rounds or Petrol Burners card puts a switch into each Mk3's panel, so you convert a tower when its new supply line stands.

Towers charge a special. Right-click a charged tower to fire it, selected or not (or select it and press Space).Hold a charged tower, or double-tap it, to fire it (or tap it and use Fire special). The tower then reboots for a few seconds.

A campaign lasts 20, 25 or 30 waves depending on the threat level. A Colossus arrives every 5th wave. Survival has no last wave. A machine that reaches the Heart stops and attacks it. Destroy it before it strikes and the core takes no damage.

Every tower that picks a target can be told which: select it and choose First, Last, Strongest, Weakest, Fastest or Slowest in its panel (T steps through them). Set all gives the mode to every tower of that kind, as does a Shift-click on a mode, and new towers of the kind start with it. A bracket marks the enemy the selected tower is aiming at.

Broodcarriers let mites loose that climb a tower and jam it: a violet ring crackles round its base with a mite over it, and any hit knocks them off. Late in a campaign the Titan lands and stuns every tower (a cold blue ring and a bolt until it reboots): its hull turns most tower fire, so save the specials for it.
